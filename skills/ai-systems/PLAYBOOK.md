## 0. The offer in one line

Lift finds the workflow costing a local business the most missed revenue, sets up an AI-powered system that catches it, proves it works, and hands over the keys — the client owns everything.

Sell the outcome, never the tool: "stop losing the calls you're missing," not "a GoHighLevel setup." (Tool-first framing invites price-shopping against $97/mo software — documented churn cause.)

## 1. Activation gates (all three, in order — no exceptions)

1. **A named buyer.** A specific business owner who has said they'd pay real money. A compliment is not a buyer. First test: ask Alyssa for one name and an intro.
2. **A discovery call that surfaces a real, scoped pain** (§4, step 1).
3. **A signed one-page scope with money attached** (§4, step 3).

A paying pilot may proceed whenever it appears — revenue is the point. What may NOT happen before all three gates pass: menu lines, website copy, tool subscriptions, spec builds, or any Megan-hours on this track during a send week. If gate 1 hasn't passed by 2026-08-07, this file just keeps sitting here quietly, costing nothing.

## 3. What Lift sells — and refuses

**The four systems with proven local demand** (scout-verified willingness-to-pay):

1. **Missed-call text-back + fast lead follow-up** — strongest evidence in the market (62% of SMB calls go unanswered; first responder usually wins the job). Cheap to run, instant to demo. The default first system.
2. **Google review automation** — text request within 30 minutes of job completion converts 25–40% vs 3–8% later. Feeds local SEO directly, which is *already Lift's Lane S product* — the natural cross-sell in both directions.
3. **AI receptionist / after-hours answering** — proven $99–$300/mo direct-to-SMB demand in trades and med spas, exactly Lift's verticals. Advanced rung only.
4. **Booking + reminder flows** — table stakes for med spas and order-driven food businesses; bundle with #1 rather than selling alone.

**Refusals:**
- **No custom "Reeves Collection" builds.** What Megan built for herself is an operating system plus an art project; clients buy one boring, reliable workflow. Anyone asking for "an AI dashboard for everything" gets scoped down to system #1–4 or referred out.
- **No standalone reporting dashboards** — no willingness-to-pay evidence at this client size. Reporting is the retention layer inside the Care Plan, not a product.
- **No ranking/traffic/ROI guarantees.** Same law as Lane S.
- **One system per engagement.** The second system is the upsell after the first one works.

## 4. The delivery protocol (start here, do this, in this order)

**Step 0 — Qualify (15 min, before agreeing to anything).** Real business, real revenue, a phone/booking/review problem they've *said out loud*, and the owner answers their own texts. Any "no" → polite pass.

**Step 1 — Discovery call (30 min, free, and it is a call).** Six questions, then stop talking:
1. Walk me through what happens when a customer calls and you can't pick up.
2. How do new customers book right now — every step, who touches it?
3. When did you last ask a happy customer for a review? What happened?
4. What's the one task you'd pay to never do again?
5. What software do you already pay for? (They always forget half — ask for the credit-card view.)
6. If this works perfectly, what number changes — bookings, reviews, hours back?
The answer to Q6 becomes the success metric in writing. Do NOT diagnose or design on this call.

**Step 2 — Workflow audit (1–2 hrs, Megan's desk).** Map the one broken workflow end-to-end: where leads enter, where they die, what it costs monthly (their numbers, not invented ones — every claim sourced from what THEY said or something checkable, per the Lift claim standard). Pick the ONE system (§3) with the shortest path from "installed" to "owner sees money." Output: one page.

**Step 3 — Scope one-pager (the contract).** Exactly one page: the system in plain English · what's included and NOT included · the success metric from Q6 · all-in monthly tool cost (itemized, paid by client) · Lift's price from §2 · timeline (2 weeks install for Simple) · support terms in writing: **business-hours, best-effort; no SLA, no 24/7, no uptime promise** (a Saturday-6pm booking failure with no written terms makes Megan the unpaid on-call vendor of record — this line is what prevents that) · handoff = client owns and operates everything. Signed + 50% deposit before any build hour.

**Step 4 — Build (on THEIR accounts, from day one).** Every account — platform, phone number, Google profile, payment — is created under the client's email with the client's card. Megan works from an invited staff/admin seat that can be revoked. **Nothing a client pays for ever runs on Megan's personal logins** — that's the house identity firewall, exported. Default stack, chosen on merit: GoHighLevel sub-account (~$97–$150/mo light use) for missed-call/booking/reviews; NiceJob ($75–$125/mo) if reviews-only; purpose-built receptionist tools (PickupBell/CallBird class, $99–$199/mo) for voice. Simplest tool that does the job wins; fewer moving parts = fewer 9pm breakages.

**Step 5 — Test like it faces customers, because it does.** Fire the real flows with a real phone before the client ever sees it: call and don't answer → does the text arrive, does it read like a human who works there? Book, reschedule, cancel. Send the review request. Every customer-facing message passes `_system/LIFT_ANTI_AI_OUTPUT_QA_STANDARD.md` — nothing that reads as AI reaches a customer, in any channel. Voice flows get called ten times, including the weird cases (wrong number, angry caller, kid). Then the owner tests it themselves for 2–3 days before it goes live.

**Step 6 — Handoff (the part competitors skip; Lift's signature).** A plain-language owner's guide — one page, zero jargon: what it does, what a normal day looks like, the three things that can go wrong and the one button that fixes each, who to call for tool problems (the vendor) vs. system problems (Lift). Plus a 15-minute recorded screen walkthrough. Then a live 30-minute training where the OWNER drives. Done means: the owner can explain the system to their own staff without Megan in the room.

**Step 7 — 30-day check (included in setup price).** One call at day 30: pull the numbers against the Q6 metric ("14 missed calls caught, 9 new reviews"). If the system survived the month without intervention → the Care Plan unlocks (§2) and gets offered ONCE, in outcome language, no pressure. If it needed hands-on fixes → fix the design first; do not sell recurring management on a fragile build.

**Step 8 — Care Plan delivery (only after §2 unlock).** Monthly: monitoring check, break-fixes within scope, and a 5-line ROI email in business terms — calls caught, reviews gained, hours saved. Clients cancel when they can't connect the fee to outcomes; the report IS the retention. Quarterly: one improvement suggestion. Support terms from Step 3 carry over unchanged.
