## Sharpest positioning (one sentence)

"I looked at your website, and here's exactly why people who search for [your service] in [your town] aren't calling you — and what I'd do about it." Not "boutique studio," not "digital presence" — the evidence-based fixer for local search invisibility. The blueprint's positioning is right; this is its bluntest form. Sell the finding, then sell the fix.

## Best first verticals — NARROW the Tier A list

Tier A (5 verticals × 9 geos) is still too wide for week one. **Pick two verticals, three geos:**
- **HVAC — first.** It is July. Peak AC-emergency season in central PA. "Emergency AC repair near me" search volume is at its annual maximum RIGHT NOW, and every HVAC owner is living it. Timing beats everything.
- **Plumbing — second.** Year-round emergency demand; same owner profile; sustains the pipeline when HVAC seasonality cools.
- **Geos:** Harrisburg, Mechanicsburg, York (largest of the core list). Expand only after 20 sends.
Two verticals × three geos = pattern recognition in days instead of weeks. Roofing/pest/electrical stay Tier A for weeks 3–4.

## Best first offer — restructure the ladder's front door

The $250 Mini-Audit as "flagship" has a conversion problem: cold Tier A owners don't buy audits from strangers; they buy outcomes. Sharper mechanic:

1. **Free hook stays** (one specific lite-audit observation in the first email).
2. **Mini-Audit $250, repositioned as a credited tripwire:** "$250, and it's credited toward any package if you move forward within 30 days." Feels near-free to the buyer, filters tire-kickers, protects Megan's hours, and builds the upsell into the purchase itself.
3. **First real close target: Blog & SEO Foundation ($600–1,200) with month one of Blog Essentials bundled** (~$1,100–1,950 first invoice, then $500–750/mo). One close ≈ the entire first-$1,000 goal.
The retainer remains the profit core. The Mini-Audit is the tripwire, not the flagship.
